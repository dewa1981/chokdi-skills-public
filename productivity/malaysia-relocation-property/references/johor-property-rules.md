# Johor / Malaysia property rules for a non-citizen buyer

Verify against the state land office (PTG Johor) and the developer's licence before any money moves. Numbers below are the last verified values, not permanent truth.

## Price floors (non-citizen)

- Strata (condo, serviced apartment, SoHo): **RM1,000,000 minimum** — per unit, applies to new launches and sub-sales alike.
- Landed (terrace, semi-D, bungalow): **RM2,000,000 minimum** per the 2026 guides; other guides quote RM1,000,000. The conflict is real — get the applicable floor **from the state, in writing**, before a booking fee.
- The floor is a **price test on the specific unit**, not on the project. A development whose ceiling is below the floor has no unit a foreigner can ever buy.
- The floor also binds the **exit**: a later sale to another foreigner must again clear the floor.
- Federal minimum sits underneath every state: below RM1,000,000 per unit is not acquirable by a foreign interest anywhere.

## Fee stack on top of price (Johor, non-citizen, residential)

- MOT stamp duty: flat **8%** of price (from 1 Jan 2026; Malaysian PRs excluded).
- Johor state consent levy: **3% or RM30,000, whichever is higher** (from 1 Jul 2025; 2%/RM20,000 applied to agreements lodged on or before 29 Aug 2025).
- Serviced apartment under RM1m: levy minimum rises to **RM50,000**.
- Industrial: levy 4%, no minimum, and no 8% duty.
- State consent (Kebenaran Pindah Milik): s.433B National Land Code, written approval required; s.433C makes an unconsented dealing **void**.
- SPA legal fees: roughly 1.25% on the first RM500,000 and 1% above, plus 8% SST and disbursements.
- Loan agreement: stamp duty 0.5% of loan plus legal fees.
- Financing: 60–70% LTV typical for a non-citizen.

Worked reference: on a RM1,050,000 strata purchase the statutory outlay before legal fees is about RM120,000 (~11.5% of price).

## Closed to non-citizens at any price

Malay reserve land; units allocated to Bumiputera interests in a development; low-cost and medium-low-cost housing as designated by the State Authority. Johor also applies a Bumiputera allocation, so a *specific* unit can be unavailable for reasons unrelated to price or passport.

## Known exceptions

- **Medini (Iskandar Puteri)**: new strata units bought direct from the developer have no state minimum. Does not extend to central JB or Bukit Chagar.
- Individual state-approved developer phases (e.g. specific Forest City phases) — confirm per phase, never from an agent's sentence alone.

## Verification checklist before paying anything

1. Extract from the documents the user has: developer registered name, company registration number, project name, phase/block, plot number (PTO) and title number (H.S.(D) or equivalent).
2. Check the registration number on the invoice matches the SPA exactly.
3. Check the project's **APDL** (Advertising Permit & Developer Licence) status via KPKT.
4. Ask whether the lot is a **Bumi lot** and, if so, for the release letter.
5. Read the title's **Sekatan Kepentingan** (restriction in interest) field — it is what forces state consent.
6. Confirm the **HDA account** name equals the developer's registered name; pay nothing to an individual.
7. For progress billing, confirm the stage matches the attached **architect's certificate**.

## Practical advice for a mid-market buyer (stock RM300k–800k)

Below-floor stock cannot be bought in a foreign name. The workable options are: rent; or the Malaysian-citizen relative buys and lets it to the foreign buyer on a documented tenancy. Never suggest nominee or shareholding arrangements — they expose the buyer and are frequently structured illegally.
