# Paying for a Thai home with crypto / a partner's money — source-of-funds playbook

Use when the buyer pays **cash (no mortgage)** and the money arrives as crypto sold for
baht, or as transfers from a partner/relative. A cash deal moves the entire "prove where
the money came from" burden onto the buyer: **no bank filters the payment**, while
กรมที่ดิน and the receiving bank still ask, and the AML reporting threshold for
cash-equivalent flows sits around **5 million THB** — right where a 2.5–5M house lands.

Bridge the gap to the price she actually wants with the three levers below, in this order:
shrink the donor ask (Rule 2), liquidate her own gold, then stretch the payment with the
developer's installment plan.

## Rule 1 — the money path must read as ONE line

**Donor → exchange/wallet in HER name → sold for THB → her own bank account →
cashier's cheque / transfer in her name → developer.**

Never: cash, a third party's account paying the developer, a wallet in someone else's
name, or large P2P trades with strangers (frozen account + funds of unknown origin).
Tell the user to keep a **separate bank account** for this money so it never mixes with
daily spending — it is far easier to explain later.

Once a tranche has been sold into baht it carries **no FX risk and no issuer risk**, and the
bank statement that received it is the closing link of the chain — say both, because buyers
usually experience the conversion as pure loss.

## Rule 2 — documents, both sides

**From the donor (the source of funds):**
- ID/passport copy (signed true copy) — a phone photo is normally accepted.
- **Gift letter** — signed, stating the amount, the date, the purpose (residential
  property for herself and her child) and the words *by way of gift, with no
  consideration and no expectation of repayment*.
- **Donor's declaration of source of funds** — one page, TH/EN, signed, naming his income
  sources in his own words: rental income from property he owns abroad, investment/trading
  income (securities, digital assets), salary/business income as applicable, with a statement
  that the funds are lawful and he will cooperate on request. This is the instrument that
  replaces historical broker/exchange records donors can rarely produce.
- Proof of upstream income. When the source is **crypto trading, not salary**, the top
  layer is the trading record: exchange **trade history / account statements (2–3 years,
  CSV or PDF)**, evidence of the **initial capital** that bought the first crypto
  (bank slip / on-ramp slip — the weakest link in every crypto case: collect whatever
  exists), the **TxID list of every transfer to her**, and any tax returns he filed.

**From the buyer:**
- Exchange account **in her own name**, KYC complete.
- Receipt history: date · amount · TxID · **THB value on the receipt date — that value is
  the cost basis**.
- Off-ramp (sell) statements + bank statements showing exchange → her account → developer.
- **Buyer's own declaration of source of funds** — one page, TH/EN, signed: the funding routes
  (gift from a non-registered partner, THB proceeds from a licensed exchange, own bank savings
  and/or a gold sale) plus a ticked list of the attached documents. It supports, never replaces,
  the statements themselves.
- A crypto ledger with the fields the Revenue Department requires (coin, date/time,
  transaction type, quantity, price, THB value, fees, cost) — keep it 10 years.

## The minimum ask that unblocks the purchase

A donor who is abroad, busy or disorganised will not dig out 2–3 years of broker statements —
and the purchase does not actually require it. Shrink the request to what is both sufficient and
painless: **(1) gift letter signed, (2) donor's declaration of source of funds signed, (3) a phone
photo of his ID/passport.** Say it that way when asking ("เซ็น 2 หน้า + ถ่ายรูปบัตร") so the request
reads as small; keep the tiers under "Recovering documents" in reserve for a follow-up question
from an officer. The buyer's side is the heavy half and she can always produce it herself — lead
with that, because "เอกสารจากแฟน" is what makes her want to give up on the whole house.

## When asked to buy without any paperwork fuss — rank the options, don't lecture

The recurring question is "วิธีไหนที่ซื้อบ้านโดยไม่ต้องวุ่นวายกับเอกสาร". Answer as a ranked list
from least to most fuss, with the reason attached:
1. **Sell her own gold bars → buy the project house.** One shop receipt, no third party involved.
2. **Buy with cash she already holds + save the freshly transferred money into clean records**
   (each new tranche documented from day one — no history to reconstruct). Slower.
3. **Part-mortgage / part-cash** — the bank carries the proof burden for its share, but a
   gift is not income, so the bank's income test is the real obstacle: check it before promising.
4. **Self-build** — is *not* the low-paperwork option; see the SKILL.md pitfall.
And one prohibition: never suggest splitting payments or accounts to stay under the ~5M line —
that is structuring, it is the thing that gets accounts frozen.

## Rule 3 — tax ceilings (ask the marital status BEFORE quoting numbers)

- **ม.42(17) gift exemption, per year:** from a **registered spouse 20M THB**; from
  anyone else (partner, sibling, friend) **10M THB**. Partner-with-a-child-together is
  still "not a spouse" if they never registered. A 2.5–5M house sits inside either
  ceiling — but only if it is a genuine gift, not disguised income.
- **Crypto gains = ม.40(4)(ฌ)**: taxable on the amount above cost, cost = value when
  received. Receiving and selling at nearly the same rate keeps the gain tiny — but only
  if the **receipt date and price were recorded**; without that, nothing can be computed.
  A licensed Thai exchange withholds 15% at source (เก็บ 50 ทวิ) or she files ภ.ง.ด.90;
  VAT is 0%. A **foreign** wallet falls under ม.41 (≥180 days in Thailand + remittance in
  the same year).
- Tell the user plainly, without moralising: the gift is exempt **for her**, but if the
  upstream crypto gains were never declared, an enquiry lands on **him** — one paid hour
  with an accountant is the cheap fix.
- Ownership side effect worth stating: **if they are not registered and the house is in
  her name, the house is her asset** — the signed gift letter is what protects that claim.

## Liquefying the buyer's own assets — gold bars first

When she asks "ควรเอาทองแท่งไปขายดีมั้ย", the answer is usually *yes for the shortfall*: gold is the
only funding source whose paper trail needs **no third party at all** — she already owns it and the
shop receipt proves its origin by itself. What to check, in order:

- **Unit math:** **1 บาททอง = 15.244 g**. Quotes are per บาททอง from the Thai Gold Traders
  Association (goldtraders.or.th) and re-announced several times a day — read the **ราคารับซื้อ**
  (buy-back) line, not ขายออก, and time the shop visit to a high announcement.
- **Sell the shortfall plus a small buffer, keep a reserve tranche.** Gold is her
  inflation/emergency hedge and a house is a one-time need; selling it all trades one kind of
  security for another.
- **Bars are the cheap thing to liquidate:** same-day spread on ทองคำแท่ง 96.5% runs ≈0.3%,
  against a 5–10% haircut on ทองรูปพรรณ. Never suggest melting ornaments when bars are held.
- **Tax posture** for an individual disposing of personal gold bars (not a trade/business):
  generally no income tax on the gain and no VAT; if she sells repeatedly as a business or the
  amounts are large, one paid accountant hour. Re-verify against rd.go.th before quoting.
- **How to sell:** a shop with a physical storefront, ideally the one that sold it; weigh and
  count the cash in front of her; keep **both** receipts (original purchase + sale) — the pair is
  the evidence bundle. Never buy/sell through a social page or an online middleman.
- Bank-issued 99.99% bars / gold-savings apps: sell back to the issuer or through the app, which
  pays better than a general shop and emits its own statement.
- State the alternatives in the same answer: a **gold-backed loan** (~6–7% p.a.) keeps the gold but
  creates debt, and her **unused land** can be sold instead — price a rural Rai from listing
  portals (Mae Sai: ติดถนนพหลโยธิน ≈1.6–3.5M/rai; agricultural land off-road ≈0.29M/rai) and
  subtract transfer fee 2% + specific business tax 3.3% (held <5 yrs; 1% withholding if ≥5 yrs)
  + 0.5% stamp duty before quoting a net figure.

## When the house costs more than the cash on hand — developer installments (ผ่อนดาวน์)

Her reachable price rises by stretching the payment, not by cutting the price. Ask the developer
**before any deposit** (these are also the questions to hand her as a script):
1. "ถ้าซื้อสดปลายทาง ไม่กู้ธนาคาร ขอผ่อนดาวน์ 12–24 เดือนได้ไหม" — many projects run ~0%
   installments on a 10–30% down payment with the balance due at transfer.
2. What is included (ค่าโอน, ของแถม, ค่าส่วนกลาง) and what the **transfer-date window** is.
3. The two project-safety questions (ถมดินสูงกว่าระดับน้ำท่วมปีล่าสุดกี่ซม.; ออกแบบตาม มยผ.1301/1302 ไหม).

Worked shape (5.5M THB unit): 10%/12 months, 15%/18 months and 20%/24 months all land at
≈46,000 THB/month — the monthly is nearly constant, so choose the schedule by **how much cash must
remain at transfer**, not by the monthly figure. Put that schedule next to her real saving rate
(a partner's 8,000 USDT/month ≈ 268k THB at ≈33.5) and show the remaining gap and the month it
closes; then list gold / land / part-mortgage as the levers that shorten it. Two hard rules:
**no large deposit while the funds are not yet in place** (project deposits are typically
non-refundable) and **negotiate the transfer date to a future month at contract stage** — it is
near-impossible to move after signing.

Checklist to verify before the meeting: the amount she can actually save per month (not the
amount transferred), how much gold she holds, whether the unused land is saleable, and whether she
accepts any debt at all. Without those four answers every timeline is a guess — say so rather than
presenting one.

## Rule 4 — documents for the transfer day

ID card + house registration (buyer) · sale-and-purchase agreement + developer receipts ·
payment evidence in her name (cashier's cheque/transfer) · spouse's consent + marriage
certificate only if registered · plus a second copy of the funding set, because the land
office or bank may ask on the spot.

## Deliverables that worked (build these)

1. **`.docx` checklist** — money path diagram, donor documents, buyer documents, transfer-day
   documents, the tax section, a ledger column template, a blank gift letter, do/don't list,
   and a countdown timeline (2–3 months out: start converting in tranches ≈ ≤500k THB each;
   1 month: bank + accountant consult, collect donor papers; 2 weeks: one PDF bundle with an
   index; transfer day: originals + 2 copies).
2. **`.xlsx` ledger** — receipt/sale log (quantity × price → THB, cost, TxID), auto-summary
   (SUMIF per side, average sale rate, remaining balance, estimated gain), document checklist,
   budget estimate, timeline.
3. **Bilingual TH/EN gift letter + document request** addressed to the donor, so the ask does
   not read as distrust.
4. **Bilingual TH/EN buyer's declaration of source of funds** — the one-page form that gets
   signed and attached when an officer asks.
5. **Bilingual TH/EN donor's declaration of source of funds** — same, for the donor's side.
6. **`.xlsx` monthly funding plan** when the target price exceeds the cash on hand: rate and
   monthly-transfer cells as inputs, then cash-on-hand + each month's conversion → running total
   vs. the true budget line (price + fees + furnishing + reserve), with the month the gap closes.
   Keep the inputs editable — she will re-run it whenever the FX rate moves.

Keep every deliverable as a **re-runnable generator script** (`/opt/data/scripts/_make_*.py`,
`python-docx` with Tahoma for Thai + `openpyxl` for ledgers) and send it with `MEDIA:/abs/path`.
These documents get re-requested weeks later and the profile's caches get cleared — re-running the
script with a corrected input beats rebuilding the document by hand, and a stale path inside it is
the usual failure to fix.

## Pitfalls

- **Verify tax ceilings against rd.go.th / the current announcement — never a blog.** The
  gift ceiling, the crypto record-keeping announcement and the AML threshold all move.
- **Re-open a generated `.xlsx` with openpyxl and assert formula↔row alignment before
  sending.** Relative refs drift by a row (average-rate pointing at the remaining-balance
  cell, gain subtracting the wrong line) and the totals only look slightly odd — the user
  cannot tell it is wrong.
- **One legal source per claim.** Gift ceilings and the crypto treatment come from the
  Revenue Department; developer-transfer rules from กรมที่ดิน; exchange/source-of-funds
  behaviour from the exchange's own published statement process.
- **Do not promise a "clean" tax answer.** Say what is exempt, what is taxable, and that a
  one-off accountant visit is the way to close it.
- Convert in **tranches**, not one 3–5M swap: less spread/price risk and a monotonous,
  easy-to-explain paper trail (risk management, not concealment — say so).
- **Never split payments or accounts to stay under the ~5M reporting line.** Walking the
  threshold is structuring; the consequence is a frozen account, not less paperwork.
- **Do not answer the paperwork question with a property suggestion.** "เอกสารยุ่งจัง ขอสร้างเองดีไหม"
  is a request for a route through the documents, not for a different house or a different
  construction method.

## When the donor's money crosses borders — the upstream layer

Money that starts abroad (e.g. **shares sold in one country, banked in another currency, then
turned into USDT**) has one evidence layer per hop, and each hop belongs to a different tax
authority. **Ask for the donor's tax residence first** — Indonesia / Thailand / Singapore —
because it decides who owns the upstream income; the buyer's own exposure does not change
(the gift ceiling and her own crypto gain are separate).

Document ask, one row per hop: broker **contract note** (share sale) → **receiving bank
statement in that currency** → **exchange purchase/withdrawal record + withholding slip** →
**TxIDs** of every transfer → recipient-side off-ramp and bank statements. Ship it as a
one-page money-path diagram with a table of "hop → document → who provides it → status"; that
cover sheet is what gets handed to a bank or the land office.

Jurisdiction notes worth knowing (re-verify before quoting):

- **Singapore**: no capital gains tax — the share-sale hop is untaxed there.
- **Indonesia, crypto (PMK 50/2025, in force for FY2026)**: the seller pays **PPh 22 final
  0.21%** of transaction value when trading through a domestic (OJK-licensed) PPMSE, which
  **withholds it and issues a withholding slip (bukti potong)**; VAT on crypto transfers was
  **removed**. A foreign PPMSE appointed as collector withholds **1%**; if it is not
  appointed, the seller self-remits 1%. Practical punchline: **a donor who traded on a
  registered Indonesian exchange has probably already paid and can download the slip** —
  often he does not know, so ask for the slip before assuming a problem.
- **Indonesia, shares**: the 0.1% final rate applies to **IDX-listed** shares only. Gains on
  **foreign-listed** shares (Singapore included) held by an Indonesian tax resident are
  reportable in the annual SPT at progressive rates up to 35% — that is the one layer to flag
  as a real risk, and it is **his** filing, not hers.

## Crypto accumulated and never sold — cost basis, and "can I sell it all at once?"

Monthly USDT received and never sold is normal ("ฉันไม่ค่อยได้ขาย"). Two consequences:

- **The cost basis is reconstructible without the donor's paperwork.** Export the wallet/exchange
  deposit history (date · amount · **TxID**) and price each row at the **USD/THB rate on the
  receipt date** (Bank of Thailand / commercial-bank historical rates). The ledger can be built
  retroactively — say so, because "เอกสารหาย" feels fatal to the user and this part is not.
- **USDT is USD-pegged, so the "gain" is FX.** If the baht strengthened since receipt, the sale
  realises a **loss** — no tax due, and it offsets gains in the same year. Either way the
  receipt-date record is what makes it computable.

"Can I sell it all in one go?" — legal, but push back with the mechanics, not a rule: one
3–5M THB inflow is a single **large-transaction pattern** at the bank (and the ~5M AML line),
exchange **withdrawal limits per day/per transaction** can stop the money arriving in time, and
holding a USD-pegged stablecoin while the purchase target is priced in baht is unflagged FX +
issuer risk. Recommended shape: **sell 30% early to start the paper trail, keep the rest until
the price of the house is known, then sell the remainder in 4–8 tranches 2–3 weeks before the
transfer date** (≈ ≤500k THB per tranche).

## Cashing out to baht — the exchange side, and what the transfer really costs

The exchange decides whether the money arrives on time. Verify these **in the account**, not from a blog:

- **The real daily withdrawal limit lives in the withdrawal screen**, not on the loyalty page. A
  "Privilege / Tier Gold / cumulative trading volume" screen is a rewards tier and tells you nothing
  about cash-out capacity — the number that matters is shown on the THB-withdrawal screen
  ("วงเงินการถอนต่อวันคงเหลือ") or the exchange's web account-level page. Published limit figures drift
  between announcements (the same exchange has published 650k, 2M and 50M per day at different times),
  so read the limit from the account or ask support — and say plainly that you are confirming rather
  than quoting a stale figure.
- **A long, consistent trading history is itself evidence.** A multi-year statement history makes a
  large sale unremarkable to a reviewing officer; users rarely realise their own record is this
  strong. Say it, because the paperwork is what makes them want to abandon the purchase.
- **Cost stack to quote up front** (verify current rates): trading fee ≈0.25% of the sold value
  (≈12.5k THB on 5M), a per-withdrawal bank fee (tens of baht), and the 15% withholding on the *gain*
  — which is creditable: where the seller's other income sits below that rate, filing ภ.ง.ด.90 with
  the gain included can reclaim it, so one paid accountant hour is worth it at this volume.
- **Keep each tranche under the ~5M THB reporting line while declaring everything.** Splitting to
  *avoid* reporting is structuring; scheduled tranches whose purpose is stated to the bank are
  ordinary risk management. State the distinction out loud, or the two rules read as contradictory.
- **Warn the receiving bank 2–3 weeks ahead.** The branch — not the exchange — is where a ~5M inflow
  gets questioned, and the regulator's joint scrutiny of large USDT inflows makes an announced,
  documented purpose (buying a home) the cheapest protection available. Ask the branch manager for the
  document list in writing; some banks issue a reference letter that satisfies the developer.
- **Keep the Thai SIM active while abroad.** Every sale and withdrawal needs an OTP — a dual-SIM phone
  with the Thai SIM live is a prerequisite, not a convenience.

## Budgeting the cash need: check the fee measure before using 2%

Thai transfer/mortgage registration fees follow government stimulus measures, and while a measure is in
force the registration fee on a residence at or below the ceiling (7M THB) drops to **0.01%** — on a 5.5M
house ≈550 THB instead of the standard 2% (≈110,000). Confirm the measure is still running and what the
price ceiling is before writing "ค่าโอน 2%" into a budget, and re-check it at contract stage: a stale 2%
line overstates the cash she needs by six figures and makes an affordable house look impossible.

## Recovering 1–2-year-old documents ("หาเอกสารยากอยู่นะ")

Tier the ask so a missing broker statement does not stall the whole purchase:

- **Tier 1 — usually sufficient in practice**: signed gift letter + **donor's declaration of source
  of funds** + donor ID (photo is fine) + **TxID list** (blockchain records never expire) + the
  buyer's own exchange and bank statements.
- **Tier 2**: exchange trade/account history (registered exchanges keep 3–5 years, self-service
  export) + foreign bank statements (request a back-dated statement; some banks hold 3–5 years).
- **Tier 3**: broker contract notes + the donor's tax filings for that year.

Recovery moves: search the donor's **email** for `contract note`, `trade confirmation`,
`statement`, `withdrawal` — brokers and exchanges mail these even when the portal is long
closed; re-login to the old portal and export the full history; render TxIDs to PDF from a
blockchain explorer; if the set is still incomplete, write a **one-page source-of-funds
statement** that narrates the hops and attach whatever exists — a consistent partial set beats
silence.

**Alternative that solves it cleanly:** the recurring monthly transfers are themselves a
buildable trail. Funding the purchase from freshly received money (each tranche with complete
records) removes the need to reconstruct history — offer that as the low-risk option, and be
honest about the trade-off (delay vs. paperwork depth).
