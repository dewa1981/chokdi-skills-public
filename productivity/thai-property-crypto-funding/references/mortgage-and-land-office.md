# Mortgage-assisted purchase, land-office scrutiny and nominee risk

Use when the buyer could pay cash but the transfer is at risk of stalling, or when a
salesperson/bank proposes a 50% loan.

## Why a loan ever helps

The land office increasingly asks where a large cash sum came from, and an officer may invite the
buyer into a room and interview them. Two facts make that worse: the buyer has no provable
income, and a foreign partner is involved. A bank that has approved a loan has already verified
the down payment, so the file reads as an ordinary financed purchase. Every sensible plan raises
the share of the price that a bank has vouched for and keeps the rest provably the buyer's own.

## Money: cash vs 50% loan (worked example, THB 5.5M house)

| Item | Cash | 50% loan (2.75M), same-day transfer + mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer registration | 0.01% ≈ 550 | 0.01% ≈ 550 |
| Mortgage registration | none | 0.01% ≈ 275 (instead of 1% ≈ 27,500) |
| Loan agreement stamp duty | none | 0.1% of loan ≈ 2,750 |
| Appraisal fee | none | often waived on developer-partner promos — get it in writing |
| Fees on transfer day | ≈ 550 | ≈ 3,600 |
| Interest, 1 / 2 / 3 years @5.5% | 0 | ≈ 151,250 / 302,500 / 453,750 |
| Discharge at payoff | none | ≈ 50 THB per plot + 5 THB request |

So fees are noise; **time in the loan is the cost**. Repaying inside the promo window is the
whole point of the plan, which is why the prepayment clause outranks every other term.

### The 0.01% measure (verify before quoting)

- Transfer fee 2% → 0.01% **and** mortgage registration 1% → 0.01%, but only when the transfer and
the mortgage are registered **in the same transaction**.
- Conditions: dwelling (detached/semi/townhouse) or condo, sale price **and** appraised value
  ≤ THB 7M, mortgage ≤ THB 7M per agreement, buyer is a **Thai national individual**.
- Window: announced 1 Jul 2026 → **30 Jun 2027**. Outside it, mortgage registration reverts to 1%
  (capped at THB 200,000), which is why the phrase "we'll register the mortgage with the transfer"
  matters.
- Withholding tax, specific business tax and stamp duty on the sale are unchanged.

## Settle these before you plan anything

1. Does the bank lend to a single borrower with **no salary**? If not, what substitute (deposit
   pledge, co-borrower, guarantor) does it accept?
2. If the partner must co-sign, what does that do to ownership? Keep him off the title, the SPA and
   the loan; a co-signer with an ownership claim is a different and worse transaction.
3. Interest basis, promo period, rate after the promo, and the exact prepayment / rate-clawback
   wording — in the offer letter, not in chat.
4. Appraisal fee, fire insurance requirement, annual fees, discharge cost.
5. Approval lead time versus the transfer date; write the transfer date into the plan only after
   the approval timeline is known.

### Describing the partner's occupation on a bank form

Rental income from property he owns abroad is an occupation, and the label decides how the
application reads: **Property Investor** or **Landlord — rental income from owned properties**
( Malaysians also use *Buy-to-Let Investor*). Never "unemployed", "no occupation" or a bare
"investor" with nothing behind it — the last one invites "invested in what?". Support it with
tenancy agreements, 6–12 months of rent landing in an account in his name, title/SPA copies and a
one-page asset list. Accept that a Thai bank usually cannot lend against his foreign property and
usually will not count his foreign rent toward the buyer's credit — which is exactly why the plan
keeps the loan in the buyer's own name and the partner's documents merely prove he is real and
solvent, not that he is a co-owner.

## The bank's document checklist is often the wrong one

A branch hands over a one-page checklist headed **รายได้ประจำ** (regular/salaried income): payslips
and an employment letter for 6 months, house/vehicle registration, marriage/divorce/death
certificates, name-change certificate, 6 months of personal bank statements, assets such as land
titles, professional licence. For a caregiver/investor buyer with no employer, three of those lines
simply do not exist — that is not a rejection, it is the wrong template.

Say it in one sentence and ask for the right list: "ดิฉันไม่ได้เป็นพนักงานประจำ ไม่มีเงินเดือน ไม่มีสลิป
แต่มีรายได้จากการลงทุน/ทรัพย์สิน และมีหลักประกันที่ดิน+คอนโด ขอเช็กลิสต์ชุด 'ผู้มีรายได้อื่น/อาชีพอิสระ/หลักประกัน' ด้วยค่ะ".

Substitutions that are accepted in practice, line by line:

| Asked | When it does not exist | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| Payslips + employment letter | no employer | foreign tax authority's **Notice of Assessment** + tax-payment history; tenancy agreements + rent credits; 6–12 months of statements |
| Marriage certificate | not legally married | the child's birth certificate (shows family status), plus a plain statement that the couple is unmarried |
| Name-change certificate | none | say "none" |
| Personal statements 6 months | — | statements for *every* account money passes through, stamped originals |
| Professional licence | not a licensed profession | not applicable — say so |

## Guarantor evidence, strongest first

For a foreign partner who lives on rent and investments (no salary), rank the proof and tell the
user which single document closes the file:

1. **Foreign tax authority's Notice of Assessment (NOA)** — the one page that states assessed
   income. A tax portal's *Statement of Account* is **not** the NOA: it lists assessments, payments
   and penalties only. Say that difference explicitly, because users believe they already sent it.
   Reading the annual tax back into income is possible (progressive brackets) but present it as an
   estimate with the bracket assumption, never as a figure the document states.
2. **Tenancy agreement(s)** — must show premises address, 12-month term, monthly rent, security
   deposit, the payment date, and a receiving account in the owner's own name. Cross-check that the
   account number in the TA is the account whose statement shows the rent credits; that single match
   is what turns a transfer labelled `OTHER`/`RENT` into provable income.
3. **Rent landing in the statement**, month by month, matching the TA amount (a small premium over
   the contracted rent is normal — ask, do not silently normalise it).
4. **Title/ownership certificate** — in Singapore a *Subsidiary Strata Certificate of Title* showing
   **Estate in Fee Simple** and **Interest/Encumbrance: Nil** is strong: freehold and unencumbered.
   Page numbers matter — the certification page proves issuance only; the applicant needs the pages
   carrying the proprietor's name, lot number, area and share value.
5. **Net-worth / asset list** roll-up of the above, plus property tax or assessment bills.

A foreign title that has been *issued* is not yet an asset the file can claim: if the developer's
letter asks for the Instrument of Transfer to be executed within N days, compute the deadline from
the letter date and state how many days overdue it is instead of describing the property as owned.
Then give the user three things in one go — call/email the conveyancing firm for the outstanding
document list and fees, ask the developer for a written statement of account and a no-arrears
confirmation for maintenance/sinking fund, and request a certified copy of the title in the owner's
name plus a letter confirming the property is unencumbered. Ask for the letter to name the owner
**in full as per passport**; a letter addressed to a single mononym (common for Indonesian and some
Singapore documents) will stall identity verification at a Thai bank even though the name is correct.

Escalating penalties in the tax payment history (repeated 5% late-payment charges, even when every
assessment was eventually paid in full) are a character signal a credit officer may read. Do not
hide them; prepare one sentence — "paid in full every year, paid late in some years, no arrears".

### If the guarantor's income is rent from rooms

Confirm the rental is legal before it becomes evidence: a co-living/room-let unit needs each
occupant's valid pass (a tenant list with pass type and expiry is printed in a proper TA), the
jurisdiction's minimum-stay rule (Singapore private residential: 3 months since Jan 2024) and its
occupancy cap (6 unrelated persons). An illegal sublet discovered at verification damages the
application more than having no rent evidence at all.

## Manage the file as an index, not a pile

Once 30+ documents are in play, generate a status workbook and regenerate it as new files arrive:
`0. สรุปสถานะ` (counts of มีแล้ว / มีแต่ต้องขอฉบับจริง / ยังขาด + the five documents that must be in
hand before the meeting) · `1. ฝั่ง buyer` · `2. ฝั่ง guarantor` · `3. รายได้รวม` (per-source income,
currency, ≈THB, and *which documents actually prove it*). Status columns drive row colour so the
gaps are visible at a glance. Keep the generator under `/opt/data/scripts/_make_*.py`.

In the chat, separate "potential income if every source is documented" from "income the bank will
count today" — the first number flatters, the second is the one that decides the loan.

## Appraisal day (the bank valuing the collateral)

Two questions the user will conflate — answer them separately.

- **Who takes the photos:** the bank's appraiser does. The owner's own photo set (10–15 shots:
exterior, lobby, each room, balcony view, meters, and any crack photographed close plus in
context) is a *backup* — it settles future disputes about pre-existing damage and can be reused
when shopping several banks without a second inspection.
- **Does the appraiser enter the unit:** verify on the bank's own site; if no page states it, say
"not published — confirm by phone" rather than asserting. Bangkok Bank's own home-loan FAQ says the
branch forwards the collateral documents to the collateral department, which **นัดวันประเมิน**
(schedules an appraisal date), and that valuation rests on the land-office appraised price + market
price + liquidity of the collateral, with a decision in about 3–10 business days. That is enough to
tell the user a visit is the norm, while flagging that no published page says "enters the room".
- **Fee:** BBL's home-loan pages quote an appraisal fee of **min THB 3,000 + VAT**. When a
salesperson says "ค่าประเมินฟรี", treat it as a project–bank promotion and get it in writing; never
repeat "free" to the user as if the bank published it.
- **Consolidated statements vs the unit:** a developer's land title deed or parcel map (owner =
the developer company, area ≈ tens of sq wah) is **not** the security for a condo unit. Only the
unit's ownership certificate / SPA carries the room number, floor and usable area. Do not value a
condo from a project land parcel map, and do not run a land-office price check on it.

### Preparing the property and the paperwork

Title deed / condo ownership certificate + SPA + payment receipts + owner's ID; juristic-person
letter confirming no common-fee arrears plus the latest receipts; tenancy agreement if rented;
bank statements if asked. On the day: lights on, curtains open, water and air-con working, a clear
path to every wall, floor and balcony — clutter that blocks inspection gets written up as "cannot
verify condition" and a visibly neglected room can cost a few percent. Declare earthquake cracks
and any unauthorised modification up front: a defect the appraiser discovers after the owner hid
it is worse than one disclosed. If the owner is in another province or abroad, arrange a power of
attorney for whoever opens the door and lodge the keys in advance — and ask the bank whether the
owner must be present at all.

If the plan is to mortgage an existing property to fund the new purchase instead of using the
partner as guarantor, say plainly what that trades: simpler documents and no foreign party in the
file, at the cost of locking that property (no sale until discharge) and paying interest on it.

## Loan application lifecycle — what can still be cancelled, and who is on the form

Approval is not debt. Debt begins when the loan agreement is signed and funds are drawn. Say this
before the user panics about "can I still cancel?".

| Stage | Can the user walk away? | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Application filed, awaiting result | yes, or amend the form | none |
| **Approved, loan agreement not yet signed** | yes — decline, change the borrower/guarantor split, or go to another bank | appraisal fee only (commonly not refundable) |
| Loan agreement signed + mortgage registered | only by repaying and discharging | discharge fees + interest already accrued |
| Property transferred | no — close the account / refinance | prepayment penalty if that clause exists |

- An approval letter carries a validity window (commonly 30–90 days); if nothing is signed the offer
  simply lapses and no action is needed.
- Changing **who borrows** after approval is not an edit — the file is re-underwritten, so expect a
  fresh application. Get the borrower/guarantor split right on the first submission.
- Have the user ask, in writing, that cancelling before the loan agreement costs nothing beyond the
  appraisal fee, and whether even that is refundable.

**A salesperson may pre-fill a second application naming the foreign partner as BORROWER "just in
case the buyer does not pass".** Refuse it. A partner-borrower reads the same as a partner-owner to
a land officer (a foreigner taking the benefit of Thai land) — exactly what stalls the transfer —
and it makes him liable for a debt he gains nothing from. The only structure to submit is one
application: buyer as sole borrower, partner as guarantor. If the backup form is already signed, say
so at the next meeting and withdraw it before any loan agreement is signed; that is allowed and
routine.

When the bank's single outstanding item is a foreign tenancy agreement the buyer cannot obtain, three
moves, in order: substitute the rent credits plus the guarantor's tax history; offer an in-country
asset (the condo certificate) as primary collateral; or move to a bank that never asked for it. Do
not let one unobtainable document end the search, and tell the user plainly that a rejection here is
this bank's document policy, not a verdict on her finances.

### Two banks at once, and how to read the account officer

- Two applications is normal and does not damage a home-loan credit profile — credit-bureau enquiries
  for housing loans are not the score-killer that repeated consumer-credit applications are.
- Do **not** file a formal application just to browse terms: filing triggers the enquiry, a meeting
  where nothing is filed does not. Chat first, file with the bank the user actually prefers.
- Repeated "have you tried other banks?" from the same officer is usually a soft signal that he
  expects the case to fail, not small talk. Ask directly — "ถ้าดูจากเอกสารตอนนี้ มีโอกาสผ่านไหมคะ ถ้าน้อย
  ดิฉันจะได้ไปเตรียมที่อื่นให้เต็มที่" — and act on the answer instead of reading the tea leaves.
- Never schedule two bank meetings on one day ~3 hours apart. A serious meeting involving a foreign
  guarantor runs 1–2 hours, plus travel and a child handover; leave 4.5+ hours or use separate days,
  and volunteer that reasoning when proposing times. Keep the first-bank-then-second order (the
  stricter bank first) only if the gap is real.

## Nominee: what it is and is not

Nominee = a Thai name holding property for a foreigner's benefit to get around foreign ownership
limits. Illegal, and the property can be seized.

Not nominee: a Thai buyer buying in her own name, paying from her own account, funded by her own
savings plus an exempt gift from a non-spouse partner, where the partner gains no ownership right.
Show those four facts with documents rather than arguing:

1. THB arrives in her account (exchange sale receipts, bank statement).
2. Payment leaves her account (cashier's cheque / own-name transfer to the developer account).
3. Gift Letter states a gift with no repayment and no consideration, and the two are unmarried.
4. Nothing in the SPA gives the partner a share, a lien or a right to occupy-and-claim.

## Interview-ready folder (one PDF set on the phone, one printed set)

Source-of-funds folder already built for this class of case, plus: long-run exchange trade history
(proves genuine activity), the partner's own property/asset evidence abroad (proves he is not a
front), the bank's loan approval letter, and the donor declaration. If the officer asks for the
aggregate, give the aggregate.

## Lines that work

- To the salesperson: "ถ้าซื้อสดแล้วกรมที่ดินเรียกสอบ ทางโครงการช่วยอะไรได้บ้างคะ และถ้ากู้ 50% ต้องใช้เอกสารอะไรเพิ่ม"
- To the bank: "ขอดอกเบี้ยปี 1-3 และหลังโปรฯ · มีค่าปรับชำระคืนก่อนกำหนดหรือเรียกคืนส่วนลดไหม ขอเป็นลายลักษณ์อักษร · ค่าประเมินฟรีจริงไหม"
- To neither: never speculate about which officer will ask what. Prepare, do not rehearse a story.
